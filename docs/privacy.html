Privacy
policy.

An app this small should not need a policy this long. We wrote it anyway, so you can read it on a train and feel informed rather than tricked.

The short version: we do not run a server, we do not track you, and your car maker's password only goes to your car maker. Everything else is detail, and the detail is below.

1.Who we are, and who we are not

Bloo is an independent app that talks to the Bluelink and Connected Car services of Hyundai, Genesis and Kia. It is not made by, endorsed by, or affiliated with Hyundai Motor Company, Genesis, Kia, or any of their subsidiaries. We have never met them. They have never met us. Everyone is happier that way.

2.What the app keeps, and where

Everything the app keeps stays on your phone. There is no Bloo server to keep it on, because there is no Bloo server.

  • Your Bluelink, Genesis or Kia password, and any service PIN. Encrypted with AES-256-GCM, with the key sealed in the Android Keystore. Think of it as a diary that only the phone can read, locked in a safe that only the phone has the combination for.
  • Your sign-in tokens and the app-lock PIN. Kept in the same encrypted vault, for the same reason you do not keep your house key taped to the door.
  • Your settings, widget choices, and the last car status the app fetched. Kept in the app's private storage, which Android encrypts whenever your phone is locked.

If you turn on Drive sync, your settings (never your password) go to a file in your own Google Drive, so your other devices can read them. That file is yours. We cannot see it. We would not know what to do with it if we could.

3.Where things go

  • To your car maker's service, using your own account. This is the whole point of the app. Locking, starting, finding and reading your car all happen there. Their privacy policy governs what they do with the request, and we would read it too, if we were you.
  • To GitHub, to check whether a newer build exists. It sends no account details, no car data, and no identifier that points at you.
  • Nowhere else. No analytics, no advertising, no crash-reporting service, no tracking pixels hiding in the buttons.

The on-device AI summaries run on your phone. Nothing you summarise leaves it. The AI is a very well-read houseguest that does not talk to the neighbours.

4.Transactions

Bloo does not sell anything. There is no shop, no subscription box, no "premium" button glowing at you in the corner. Bloo does not take payments, hold money, or store card details, and it is not a bank, a car dealer, or a fairground ride.

A transaction, in the sense of "something you asked the car to do," looks like this:

  • You tap Lock. Bloo sends that request to your car maker's service, using your account. The service locks the car and reports back. That round trip is the transaction. Bloo writes down the outcome in the app so you can see it, and that is the end of our involvement.
  • You start the climate or set a charge limit. The same thing happens. Your car does the work, the car maker's service records it under their rules, and Bloo keeps only enough to show you what happened.
  • If your car maker charges you for a feature (a subscription for remote start, say), that charge happens between you and them. Bloo never sees the money, and cannot refund you, apologise on their behalf, or be blamed for the price of anything.

If you ever see a charge on your card with the word "Bloo" on it, it was not us. We would very much like to know who is impersonating us, and we would also like a word with them about their taste.

5.If a pirate takes control of your car

We are going to be honest here, because this is the part where a less careful policy would start doing jazz hands. Nobody can promise that a determined criminal will never get into your car. What Bloo does is make sure that the app is not the weak link, and that you are never left without a plan.

What protects you already:

  • Your password and PIN are encrypted on the phone, with the key held in hardware. A thief who copies your phone's files gets an expensive paperweight.
  • Your credentials go only to your car maker. Nothing is sent to a Bloo server, so there is no Bloo database for a pirate to raid. There is no Bloo database. We did mention that.
  • The app can lock itself behind your phone's own biometric or PIN lock. Turn that on. It is the pirate's least favourite setting.

What you should do if you think your car has been taken over:

  • Change your car maker's account password, from their own website or app, and turn on whatever two-step sign-in they offer. This is the single most useful thing you can do, and it works whether or not Bloo is involved at all.
  • Sign out in Bloo's Settings, which deletes the credentials the app has stored on that phone.
  • Change your service PIN in your car maker's app, if they let you. Remote commands need it, so a new PIN means the old one stops working for anyone who copied it.
  • Call your car maker. They can see the requests coming in, and they can cut off a session in ways we cannot.
  • If the car is in front of you and it is not yours to drive, call the police and the car maker. Please do not argue with the pirate. The car is not worth it.

If we learn of a weakness in the app that a pirate could use, we will fix it, say so in the change log below, and tell you what to do. We would rather you heard it from us than from a news item about a car that drove itself into a lake.

6.What we never collect

  • Your name, address, phone number, or contacts.
  • Your location. Only your car's location, and only because your car maker's service reports it to you. Your phone's own position is used on the device, to put your dot on the map, and goes nowhere.
  • Anything from other apps, or how you use your phone.
  • Money, or anything that would make a data broker's eyes light up.

7.Permissions, and why we ask

Bloo asks only for what a feature needs, and every one of them is optional. Location, to find your car and to run AutoLock. Notifications, for car alerts. Bluetooth, for AutoLock using your car's own connection. Activity recognition, so AutoLock knows when you are driving. Say no to any of them and that feature quietly sulks; nothing else breaks.

8.Your control

  • Sign out in Settings to delete your stored credentials.
  • Clear the app's data in Android's settings, or uninstall the app, to remove everything it stored.
  • Delete the sync file from your own Google Drive, if you turned on Drive sync.

There is nothing for us to export, delete, or correct, because we do not have it. This is the one time a lack of infrastructure works in your favour.

9.Children

Bloo is not for children, and we do not knowingly collect anything from them. If you are a child reading this, go and ask a grown-up to show you a car instead.

10.Changes

When this policy changes, the date at the top changes too, and the change is written down below, in plain words, with the date. We do not keep quiet edits in a drawer.
